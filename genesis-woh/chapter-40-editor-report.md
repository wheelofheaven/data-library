## The ten editorial questions — resolutions

1. **vv 1, 2 — *mashqeh* / *ofeh* + *sar ha-mashqim* / *sar ha-ofim* court-titles.** Resolved by **new central entry + commentary** (`sar-ha-mashqim-sar-ha-ofim-chief-cupbearer-baker`). One combined entry governs both base-offices (*mashqeh* cupbearer/butler, *ofeh* baker) and their matched *sar ha-X* court-titles — they are a single literary doublet within one office-title pattern, parallel to the already-central `sar-ha-tabbachim`. **Central** because *mashqeh* in the cupbearer-office sense reaches cross-corpus (Neh 1:11, Nehemiah's self-identification; 1 Kgs 10:5 = 2 Chr 9:4, the Solomonic court). ASV-baseline 'butler' / 'baker' retained; the more-literal-and-modern 'cupbearer' (the *sh-q-h* 'one who gives drink' etymology, the wine-service-and-tasting office) and the Egyptian-court-administration background (Vergote, Redford) documented in the rationale and commentary. Commentary at vv 1, 2; governs ten verses.

2. **vv 2, 7 — `saris` appliesTo scope.** Resolved by **central `appliesTo` extension**. `saris-court-official-eunuch-ambiguity` extended from `[GEN-WOH-37:36, GEN-WOH-39:1]` to add `GEN-WOH-40:2` and `GEN-WOH-40:7` — the same court-official-sense rendering ('officers') applied to the same office-title; the entry's rationale already named Gen 40:2 + 7 as extensible. Scope-formalization, not a new lexical decision. Commentary at vv 2, 7.

3. **vv 3, 4, 5 — `beit-ha-sohar` and `sar-ha-tabbachim` appliesTo scope.** Resolved by **two central `appliesTo` extensions**. `beit-ha-sohar-prison-round-house` += `GEN-WOH-40:3, 40:5` (its rationale already anticipated these as extensible); `sar-ha-tabbachim-captain-of-the-guard` += `GEN-WOH-40:3, 40:4`. Same titles, same prison, same ASV-baseline surface; scope-formalization. Commentary at v 3.

4. **vv 5, 8 — *pitron* (and *chalom*).** Resolved by **new central entry + commentary** (`pitron-dream-interpretation-yosef-cycle`). The noun *pitron* and cognate verb *p-t-r* are the defining keyword of the Gen 40-41 dream-cycle and are **near-exclusive to those two chapters in the HB** (the noun only here and at 41:11; the verb only in Gen 40-41). **Central** because the lemma saturates a clear cross-chapter cluster (Gen 40 AND Gen 41) that a single governing decision must reach; the Aramaic *pesher* cognate (Daniel) is noted. ASV-baseline 'interpretation' / 'to interpret' retained; the 'solution / decoding' nuance documented. **Confirmed the Translator's correct non-application of `divine-dream-communication-formula`**: that entry governs the dream-as-direct-divine-address clause-pattern (Gen 20:3, 31:24, where the deity speaks IN the dream and the message is plain); the Gen 40 dreams are symbolic puzzles whose hidden meaning must be decoded, a categorically different dream-type. The entry documents this distinction explicitly. Commentary at vv 5, 8, 12, 16, 18, 22.

5. **v 8 — *ha-lo le-Elohim pitronim*, the Elohim divine-name note.** Resolved by **overlay `appliesTo` extension + rationale addendum + commentary** to the existing `yosef-cycle-divine-name-alternation-yhwh-elohim` (Gen 39 overlay entry). Judged a **scope-extension of the same cycle-wide observation, not a separate locus**: Gen 40's single divine-name occurrence (v 8) is **Elohim** on Yosef's lips — exactly the cycle's Elohim-default that the Gen 39 entry documents as the norm against which Gen 39's anomalous YHWH-frame stands out (and it directly parallels Gen 39:9, *le-Elohim*, also on Yosef's lips). The entry's `appliesTo` is extended to add `GEN-WOH-40:8`, and a rationale addendum records the Gen 40:8 confirming-occurrence. Both divine names continue to render exactly as the text distributes them; the named documentary-critical scholarship (Wellhausen, von Rad, Westermann, Coats, Redford, Friedman) is carried in the entry; **no WoH synthesis** about the divine names imported (that is wiki-reserved). Commentary at v 8.

6. **vv 13, 19, 20 — *nasa rosh* "lift up the head" idiom + the dark pun.** Resolved by **new central entry + commentary** (`nasa-rosh-lift-up-the-head-idiom`). The chapter's structural keystone: *yissa et-roshekha* positive (restoration, v 13) → grimly/punningly *yissa et-roshekha me-alekha* (decapitation + hanging, v 19) → the narrator's single neutral idiom of both men at the feast (v 20), resolving oppositely. **Central** because *nasa rosh* is a cross-corpus idiom: the census-formula *se'u et-rosh* ('lift the head' = count) of Exod 30:12 / Num 1-26; the 2 Kgs 25:27 = Jer 52:31 Jehoiachin-release ('lifted up the head' = released and restored) — the closest parallel to the cupbearer's v 13 restoration. The idiom's sense-range and the deliberate Gen 40 reversal are documented. **The surface renders all three uniformly ('lift up the/thy head') so the Hebrew pun survives in English** — this is both the ASV-baseline choice and the choice that best preserves the source's literary device; any differentiated rendering would destroy the wordplay. Commentary at vv 13, 19, 20.

7. **v 15 — *bor* of the Egyptian prison + *ha-Ivrim* legacy refId.** (a) Resolved by **central `appliesTo` extension + commentary**. Yosef calls his Egyptian prison *ha-bor* — the **same noun** as the dry cistern of Gen 37:20-29 — a deliberate retrospective verbal echo. The Gen 37 `bor-pit-cistern-dry-water-shaft` entry (whose rationale already named Gen 40:15) is extended to add `GEN-WOH-40:15`; ASV-baseline 'dungeon' retained (the same lemma the Gen 37 entry renders 'pit'). Commentary documents the prison-lexicon relationship: *bor* (Gen 37; here) / *beit ha-sohar* (Gen 39-40) / *beit ha-bor* (41:14) are three terms for confinement bound by Yosef's own vocabulary; *bor* here is a synonym-by-extension (the dungeon-pit sense), not a literal pit-within-the-prison. (b) Resolved by **central refId-normalization**: `ha-ivri-hebrew-etymology` Gen 40 ref normalized `GEN-40:15` → `GEN-WOH-40:15` (targeted, consistent with the Gen 39 normalization; the entry's other legacy refs — Gen 14:13, 41:12, 43:32 — left as-is, no corpus-wide sweep). Commentary at v 15.

8. **v 6 — *zo'afim* (sad vs. agitated/dejected).** Resolved by **new overlay entry + commentary** (`zoafim-troubled-faces-cupbearer-baker-gen40`). The *za'af* root carries a stronger vexation/indignation register elsewhere (2 Chr 16:10; 26:19; Jonah 1:15; Mic 7:9), debated against the contextually-favored dejection. ASV-baseline 'sad' retained. **Overlay** because the participle is chapter-singular in the active corpus (the vexation-cluster is in Chronicles/prophets, outside the near-term corpus); noted as a seed-node for a future central *z-'-f* entry. Commentary at v 6.

9. **v 16 — *sallei chori* hapax.** Resolved by **new overlay entry + commentary** (`sallei-chori-baskets-of-white-bread-hapax-gen40`). *Chori* is a hapax legomenon of contested sense; the competing readings (white bread / white baskets / openwork-wicker) are documented and **not adjudicated** beyond noting the principal traditional reading ('white bread'). ASV-baseline 'baskets of white bread' retained. **Overlay** by definition — a hapax has zero cross-corpus reach. Commentary at v 16.

10. **v 23 — *zakhar* / *shakhach* remember-forget pairing.** Resolved by **commentary only**. The emphatic doublet (*lo zakhar ... va-yishkachehu*) is a literary cross-reference into Gen 41 (where *zakhar* returns at 41:9), answering Yosef's twofold v 14 plea (*zekhartani* / *hizkartani*) negatively and suspending him until the cupbearer remembers. No glossary entry warranted (a Gen-41-setup literary note, not a lexical decision). Both verbs preserved on the ASV-baseline surface. Commentary at v 23 (and the setup noted in the v 14 commentary).
