## Items the Reviewer should attend to

### `bnei-het-hittites-canaanite-anatolian-question` — Hittites philological debate

**Source:** בְּנֵי־חֵת / הַחִתִּי
**WoH choice:** *sons of Het* (literal, for *bnei Het* at vv 3, 5, 7,
10, 16, 18, 20) + *the Hittite* (for the gentilic-adjective *ha-Chitti*
at v 10).
**Issue:** The chapter's recurring designation for Efron's people
raises the long-standing two-Hittite problem: (a) the 2nd-millennium
BCE Anatolian Hittite Empire of Hattusa (Bryce 1998/2005, *The
Trojans and Their Neighbours* 2006; Singer; Hoffner *Hittite Myths*
1998); (b) the 1st-millennium BCE Neo-Hittite / Luwian Syro-Anatolian
successor-states (Hawkins, Bryce 2012); (c) the indigenous Canaanite
*Heth*-people of Gen 10:15 (Heth in the Table of Nations as son of
Kena'an). The modern critical consensus (Sarna, Westermann, Wenham,
Hamilton, Speiser) reads the Gen 23 *bnei Het* as (c) — indigenous
Canaanite Hebron-area population, only secondarily conflated with
the Anatolian Hittites. The older Lehmann 1953 *BASOR* 129
Anatolian-Hittite-law-parallel thesis (which read the chapter's
legal-procedure as 2nd-millennium Hittite law) has been substantially
deflated by Tucker 1966 *JBL* 85 and Westbrook 1971 *JSS* 16 in favor
of a 1st-millennium Neo-Babylonian/Neo-Assyrian conveyance-context.
**Decision taken:** translation preserves the literal *sons of Het*
(lexical-MT fidelity, neutral on the historical-identification
question) and *the Hittite* at the v 10 gentilic. Entry presents all
three identifications and the Lehmann-Tucker scholarly history.
**Recommendation:** ship. The literal *sons of Het* preserves the
lexical-MT distinction (collective-noun-phrase *bnei Het* vs.
gentilic-adjective *ha-Chitti*) and remains neutral on the wider
historical-identification debate. The entry's wider HB *Chitti/
Chittim* corpus (Gen 10:15, 26-27, 36, Num 13:29, Deut 7:1, 20:17,
Josh 1:4, Judg 1:26, 1 Sam 26, 2 Sam 11, 1 Kgs 10-11, 2 Kgs 7:6,
Ezek 16, 2 Chr 1, 8) is forward-referenced for future chapters.

### `nasi-elohim-honorific` — multiple-reading parallel; literal vs. superlative

**Source:** נְשִׂיא אֱלֹהִים
**WoH choice:** *a prince of Elohim* (literal MT preservation)
**Issue:** The Hittites' formal honorific to Avraham at v 6 admits
four parallel readings: (a) literal-theological *prince of Elohim*
(Targum Onqelos, Vulgate, Peshitta); (b) superlative-Elohim *mighty
prince* (the divine-name-superlative idiom — D. W. Thomas *VT* 3
[1953] is the classical philological treatment; cf. *harerei El* Ps
36:7, *arzei El* Ps 80:11); (c) polytheist-context (Hittite-spoken
designation in the speakers' own religious idiom); (d) diplomatic-
honorific (fixed-formula courtesy parallel to ANE *your excellency*).
The modern critical default (Sarna, Westermann, NJPS, NRSV, ASV/KJV)
is the superlative-reading (b) — *a mighty prince*. The WoH
translation preserves the literal *a prince of Elohim* (reading a),
consistent with established WoH policy on *Elohim*-bearing phrases:
where the MT preserves the lexeme, the WoH translation preserves it
rather than collapsing to an English intensifier. The construct
*n'si Elohim* is HB hapax.
**Decision taken:** literal *a prince of Elohim*. Entry presents all
four readings and documents the Thomas 1953 divine-name-superlative
idiom in detail.
**Recommendation:** ship — but flag for Reviewer attention as a
deliberate divergence from the dominant modern critical superlative-
reading. The choice is consistent with WoH policy on *Elohim*-bearing
phrases (cf. *el-elyon*, *el-roi*, *el-shaddai*, *el-olam* — all
preserved with the lexeme *El* rather than collapsed to *God*-
generics) and supports the patriarchal-narrative pattern of foreign-
recognition of the patriarch's divine-standing (parallel to Avimelekh
at Gen 21:22). The alternative (the superlative *a mighty prince*)
would also be defensible if the Reviewer prefers to follow the modern
critical default; the parallel-readings discipline of the entry would
support either choice.

### `va-yaqem-l-miqnah-legal-deed-conferment-formula` — claim_type direct or inferred?

**Source:** וַיָּקָם ... לְמִקְנָה (vv 17-18, 20)
**WoH choice:** *were made sure to Avraham as a possession*
(ASV/KJV convention preserved)
**Issue:** Editor's call between `direct` and `inferred`. The verb
*va-yaqem* (qal of *q-w-m* with the inanimate-property subject) and
the noun *miqnah* are grammatically explicit in the MT. The
form-critical reading of the chapter as a *dialogue-deed* (Tucker
1966 *JBL* 85) and as a legal-narrative reflecting ANE conveyance-
formulae (Westbrook 1971 *JSS* 16) is the modern critical consensus
and not project-distinctive synthesis. However, the precise legal-
tradition-of-origin (2nd-millennium Hittite per Lehmann 1953 vs.
1st-millennium Neo-Babylonian/Neo-Assyrian per Tucker/Westbrook)
remains debated, and the entry's identification of the formula with
the *dialogue-deed* genre carries a layer of form-critical
interpretation above the bare lexical-philological facts.
**Decision taken:** `claim_type=direct`. The lexemes are explicit;
the form-critical reading is consensus; the ANE-comparative legal-
deed parallels are documentary. Only the *which-ANE-tradition* sub-
question is contested, and the entry presents both positions.
**Recommendation:** Reviewer to confirm the `direct` call. The
alternative (`inferred`) would also be defensible if the Reviewer
prefers to emphasize that the form-critical *dialogue-deed*
classification — while consensus — is a scholarly interpretation
above the bare grammatical-explicitness threshold. The Editor's
position is that the form-critical reading has been so broadly
accepted (Sarna, Westermann, Wenham, Hamilton, Speiser all draw on
Tucker's analysis) that it qualifies as direct documentary
scholarship rather than as inferred reading; but the call is
defensibly either way.

## Reception-sensitivity items (all flagged-not-preempted)

The chapter is low reception-history compared to the dense theophany
chapters (Gen 18, 19, 22), but the Hebron-tomb tradition carries
significant uninterrupted reception-weight. The Editor has held each
reception-tradition strictly as downstream-not-preempted at every
relevant entry.

- **Jewish reception of the Hebron-tomb (Me'arat ha-Makhpelah / Qever
  ha-Avot)** — flagged at `me-arat-ha-makhpelah-cave-etymology-
  hebron-tomb`. The rabbinic *doubling*-etymology tradition
  (Genesis Rabbah 58:8; b. Eruvin 53a — including the doubled-couples
  patriarchal-burial-list of Avraham + Sarah, Yitzchaq + Rivkah,
  Ya'aqov + Le'ah, with Adam + Chavah as the foundational-couple in
  some traditions); the wider rabbinic-and-medieval Hebron-pilgrimage
  tradition; the *Sefer ha-Yashar* and *Yalqut Shim'oni* expansions;
  the medieval-Jewish guidebook-tradition (Benjamin of Tudela 12th c.,
  Petahyah of Regensburg).
- **Christian Hebron-tradition reception** — flagged at the same
  entry. The Byzantine-period church-construction at the site (the
  Basilica of Constantine, replaced by subsequent Byzantine and
  Crusader-period churches); the pilgrim-itineraries from the 4th
  century onward (Egeria, the Bordeaux Pilgrim).
- **Islamic *al-Haram al-Ibrahimi* reception** — flagged at the same
  entry. The post-conquest establishment of the Ibrahimi Mosque
  within the Herodian-period enclosure; the medieval and Ottoman-
  period architectural elaborations; the modern political-religious
  significance of the site.
- **Josephus' 1st-century-CE description** at `me-arat-ha-makhpelah-
  cave-etymology-hebron-tomb` — *Jewish Antiquities* I.237 and
  *Jewish War* IV.532, the foundational extra-biblical attestations
  of the site as the patriarchal-tomb, with the Herodian-period
  enclosure-walls (the largest Herodian construction outside
  Jerusalem) as direct material-evidence for the 1st-century-BCE
  identification.

At no point is project-distinctive WoH synthesis preempted in the
glossary entries or per-verse commentary; all WoH-specific readings
of the chapter (the patriarchal-acquisition as foundational title-
claim, the Hebron-tomb tradition as multi-generational continuity-
marker, the legal-formal seriousness of the first-purchase) are
reserved for the wiki and long-form material downstream.
