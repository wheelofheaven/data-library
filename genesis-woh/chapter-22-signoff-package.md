## The four reception traditions — all held downstream-not-preempted

The Editor maintained explicit "flagged-not-preempted" discipline on four major reception streams:

1. **Christian** — Hebrews 6:13-14 (divine self-oath), Hebrews 11:17-19 (Abraham's resurrection-belief reading of "we will return"), and substitutionary-atonement / vicarious-sacrifice typology.
2. **Jewish *Akedah*** — the liturgical centrality of the *aqedah* in Rosh Hashanah morning service; Levenson 1993 *The Death and Resurrection of the Beloved Son*; Spiegel 1967 *The Last Trial*.
3. **Islamic** — Quran 37:101-113 (son's identity disputed in Islamic tradition: Ishmael in classical Sunni, Isaac in early Islamic exegesis).
4. **Kierkegaardian existential** — *Fear and Trembling* (1843) "knight of faith" reading; Auerbach *Mimesis* (1946) ch. 1; Derrida *The Gift of Death* (1995).

**None of these readings is preempted in the translated text or in any of the new entry rationales.** The chapter reads as defensible ASV-baseline scholarly translation; the reception material is flagged but lives in the apparatus / wiki.

## The one inferred call

**`eretz-ha-moriah-temple-mount-identification` is `inferred`** (Editor's judgment). The Reviewer's note: *"The `inferred` call is the more cautious editorial signaling; the alternative `direct` would also be defensible."*

The identification rests on the Chronicler's 2 Chr 3:1 (*be-har ha-Moriah asher nirah l-David*) rather than an explicit Gen 22 datum. Modern critical scholarship (Westermann, Sarna) treats the temple-mount identification as a later editorial-harmonization. Samaritan tradition reads Mount Gerizim. LXX reads *tēn gēn tēn hypsēlēn* ("the high land") rather than a proper-noun place. The `inferred` claim_type honors this scholarly multiplicity.
