## The one item requiring human sign-off

### v 19 — *El Elyon koneh shamayim va-aretz*

**The dispute.** The Hebrew *koneh* (qal participle of *q-n-h*) can mean either "possessor" (the standard West-Semitic and biblical Hebrew meaning) or "creator" (the rarer meaning, attested in Ugaritic *qny ʾrṣ* "creator of the earth" as an epithet of El, in the Phoenician Karatepe inscription KAI 26 *El qn 'rs*, and in the Hittite-Hurrian *Elkunirsa*).

**The Editor chose "possessor"** (matching ASV). The lexical and cross-Semitic evidence supports "creator" in this divine-epithet context; modern critical consensus (NJPS, NRSV, Cross 1973, Westermann, Hamilton) favors "creator." The choice is defensible — "possessor" is a literal q-n-h gloss and the WoH preserves the Hebrew ambiguity — but it sits against the mainstream modern critical reading.

**Three options:**
1. **Approve "possessor"** (Editor's choice, ASV-traditional). Defensible philologically. Preserves ambiguity.
2. **Change to "creator"** (modern critical consensus). Stronger Ugaritic/Phoenician/Hittite epithet-evidence. Aligns with NJPS/NRSV.
3. **Render dual: "creator/possessor"** with both senses preserved in the text. Less common but defensible.

**Reviewer's recommendation:** lean toward (2) "creator" — the cross-cultural epithet evidence (Ugaritic, Phoenician, Hittite) makes the creator-reading the more philologically responsible choice in this specific divine-name context, even if "possessor" is the broader Hebrew default. But this is a minority shift away from ASV that needs your explicit sign-off.

**My recommendation:** option (2) "creator." The chapter is a West-Semitic syncretism passage where Abram is being blessed in the language of the Canaanite high-god tradition. Rendering "*El Elyon, creator of heaven and earth*" makes the West-Semitic epithet-pattern visible and matches the mainstream modern critical reading. If approved, the `koneh-shamayim-va-aretz` entry stays `direct` (lexical fact + cross-cultural attestation); the v 19 and v 22 translations get the swap.
