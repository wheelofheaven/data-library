## Reviewer-attention items (not blocking)

### `navi-first-occurrence-abraham` — MANDATORY MAJOR

**Source:** נָבִיא (*navi*) at Gen 20:7.

**Editorial weight:** the first occurrence of *navi* in the Hebrew
Bible. The title is applied to Avraham by the Elohim in a
dream-revelation to a foreign king, and the verse's paratactic
structure (*ki-navi hu v-yitpallel ba-adkha*) defines the prophetic
role at the moment of its naming: the prophet is the figure who
intercedes for the threatened party.

**Why this matters for Reviewer attention:** the entry is one of the
two highest-reception-weight first-occurrence entries in the entire
Abraham cycle to date (alongside [*hithpallel-first-prayer-occurrence*](#hithpallel-first-prayer-occurrence-mandatory-major)).
Three points to confirm:

1. The framing of the function-definition as *intercessory* (cross-corpus
   chain Exod 32 / Num 11, 14, 21 / Deut 9 / 1 Sam 7, 12 / Jer 7, 11, 14 /
   Ezek 9, 11 / Amos 7) is mainstream-scholarly and does not preempt
   project-specific synthesis. The Wheel of Heaven framing of the
   prophet-as-disclosure-channel is reserved for the wiki and long-form
   material and is *not* preempted by this entry. Reviewer should
   confirm the apparatus-only treatment.

2. The Speiser *AB Genesis* 1964 *spokesman* rendering is documented
   but not adopted; the ASV-baseline *prophet* is preserved as the
   standard scholarly rendering and as the lexically-consistent choice
   for the WoH project. Reviewer should confirm this is the right call
   given the chapter's structural pairing with the v 17 *hithpallel*
   first-occurrence.

3. The etymological dispute (the active *one who calls / proclaims*
   reading vs the passive *one who is called / summoned* reading; the
   Akkadian *nabu(m)* cognate) is summarized but not resolved; both
   readings are documented and neither preempted.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. The entry is the chapter's
single most important permanent contribution to the corpus and the
framing has been kept strictly in scholarly register.

### `hithpallel-first-prayer-occurrence` — MANDATORY MAJOR

**Source:** וַיִּתְפַּלֵּל (*va-yitpallel*) at Gen 20:17.

**Editorial weight:** the first occurrence of *hithpallel* (the
hithpael of *p-l-l*) and of the *p-l-l* root in any prayer-related
sense in the Hebrew Bible. The verse delivers the second of the
chapter's two foundational firsts, paired structurally with the v 7
*navi* designation: the prophet promised in v 7 now performs the
prophetic act that v 7 defined.

**Why this matters for Reviewer attention:**

1. The cross-corpus distribution documented in the entry is
   forward-loaded across the entire HB canon (Num 11 / 21; Deut 9; 1 Sam
   1 / 7 / 8 / 12; 1 Kgs 8 — the eleven-verse Solomonic temple-dedication
   pericope; 2 Kgs 19 / 20 = Isa 37 / 38; Jer 7 / 11 / 14 / 29 / 32 /
   37 / 42; Job 42; Dan 9; Neh 1 / 2; Chronicles). Reviewer should
   confirm the depth and breadth of cross-corpus reach is appropriate
   for a v2.17.0 entry; the *appliesTo* is anchored at Gen 20:17 only
   and will be expanded additively.

2. The Greenberg 1983 *self-assessment* etymological reading is
   documented as a minority position alongside the mainstream
   HALOT/BDB *to intercede / to pray* lexical position; both are
   preserved without preempting either. Reviewer should confirm the
   minority-position framing is appropriately weighted.

3. The translation preserves the ASV-baseline *prayed* over the more
   lexically-precise *interceded*; the function-definition is carried
   by the v 7 framing rather than by the v 17 verb's translation.
   Reviewer should confirm this is the right call given the WoH
   lexical-consistency principle.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. Like the v 7 *navi* entry,
this is a permanent first-occurrence contribution and has been
framed strictly within mainstream HB scholarship.

### `kesut-eynayim-vindication-formula`

**Source:** כְּסוּת עֵינַיִם (*kesut eynayim*) at Gen 20:16.

**Editorial weight:** a contested hapax phrase with three principal
modern readings (vindication / literal-veil / bribe). The
ASV-baseline literal *a covering of the eyes* is preserved in the
English text; the three readings are carried in the apparatus, with
the modern scholarly default (Sarna / Speiser / Skinner) selected as
the editorial-default interpretive frame in commentary.

**Why this matters for Reviewer attention:** the three-reading
dispute is genuinely live in the literature, and the editorial
default has interpretive consequences (the *vindication* reading
shapes the chapter's closing in a particular direction). The
literal preservation does not preempt the dispute, but the
commentary's interpretive frame does adopt the vindication-default
explicitly. The closing *v-et kol v-nokhachat* clause is documented
as syntactically obscure (Westermann reads possible textual
corruption); the ASV-baseline *in respect of all you are righted* is
preserved as the most defensible literal-equivalent.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. The literal preservation is
the editorially-conservative choice and allows the engaged reader to
encounter the contested idiom rather than the commentator's
resolution; the apparatus carries the dispute transparently.

### `divine-dream-communication-formula`

**Source:** וַיָּבֹא אֱלֹהִים אֶל־X בַּחֲלוֹם הַלָּיְלָה (*va-yavo
Elohim el-X ba-chalom ha-lailah*) at Gen 20:3.

**Editorial weight:** the first occurrence of the night-dream-revelation
formula in the Hebrew Bible. The clause-pattern recurs as a structural
type across Gen 28 / 31 / 37 / 40-41 and 1 Kgs 3:5, with the Num 12:6
programmatic statement framing the formula as the canonical
prophetic-revelation mode below the Mosaic face-to-face mode.

**Why this matters for Reviewer attention:** the ANE comparanda
section (Mesopotamian *šuttu* literature; Egyptian Papyrus Chester
Beatty III; Gudea's temple-building dream; Ashurbanipal's Elamite
dreams; Husser 1999; Lichtenstein 1968 JANES 1; Oppenheim 1956;
Noegel 2001) gives the entry significant cross-cultural reach.
Reviewer should confirm the comparative framing stays within
mainstream ANE scholarship and does not over-extend.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. The formula is textually
explicit, the canonical-prophetic ranking at Num 12:6 is explicit,
and the ANE comparanda are documentary.

### `b-tom-levav-u-v-niqyon-kappai-moral-innocence`

**Source:** בְּתָם־לְבָבִי וּבְנִקְיֹן כַּפַּי at Gen 20:5.

**Editorial weight:** the doubled-noun forensic-moral idiom in its
most fully-developed Pentateuchal occurrence; cross-corpus chain to
Ps 24:4, 26:6, 73:13, Job 22:30, 33:9. Reviewer should confirm the
ASV-baseline *integrity / cleanness* preservation over modern
smoothings is appropriate.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`.

### `yirat-elohim-moral-restraint-motif`

**Source:** יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים at Gen 20:11.

**Editorial weight:** the *yir'at Elohim* / *yir'at YHWH* motif is
one of the canon's most distributionally consistent moral-theological
formulae; the entry documents the cross-corpus chain (Gen 22:12
[Aqedah], Gen 42:18 [Joseph], Exod 1:17 [midwives], Exod 20:20
[Sinai], Job 1:1, Prov 1:7, Eccl 12:13) and the chapter's structural
irony (Avraham's assumption falsified by Avimelekh's actual conduct).

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. Becker 1965 *Gottesfurcht im
Alten Testament* is the standard reference.

### `atzar-rechem-closed-wombs-divine-sanction`

**Source:** עָצֹר עָצַר ... כָּל־רֶחֶם at Gen 20:18.

**Editorial weight:** the chapter's etiological coda; the doubled
infinitive-absolute construction; the cross-corpus
matriarchal-barrenness pattern (Sarai → Rebekah → Rachel → Manoah's
wife → Hannah). The 1 Sam 1:5-6 *sagar rachmah* / *sagar be-ad
rachmah* is documented as the closest verbal parallel.

**Recommendation:** ship as `direct`. Brichto 1973 *HUCA* 44 is the
standard reference; Alter's annunciation-type-scene treatment in
*The Art of Biblical Narrative* 1981 frames the canonical pattern.

## Translation register and structural observations

The chapter is largely glossary-recycling. The wife-sister type-scene
vocabulary lock from Gen 12 (the [*wife-sister-type-scene*](#wife-sister-type-scene),
[*imri-na-achoti-at*](#imri-na-achoti-at), [*elohim-as-translation*](#elohim-as-translation),
and [*eretz*](#eretz) entries) controlled most lexical decisions
without requiring new entries. The chapter's lens-relevance is
**structural rather than philological**: the foreign-king-as-moral-voice
inversion at v 4 (Avimelekh taking up Avraham's stance against
indiscriminate divine slaying from Gen 18) and the dramatic-irony at
v 11 (Avraham's *yir'at Elohim* assumption falsified by the chapter's
narrative) are the principal interpretive moves. Both are documented
in per-verse commentary in mainstream-scholarly register (Sarna,
Westermann, Wenham, Hamilton, Alter consensus); no project-specific
WoH synthesis is asserted in the translation or commentary.

The two foundational firsts (v 7 *navi*; v 17 *hithpallel*) are the
chapter's permanent contributions to the corpus. They are paired
structurally: the prophetic role is named at v 7 alongside the
prophetic function the role discharges (v 17). The chapter is the
canonical Hebrew Bible *function-definition* of the prophet as
intercessor.
