## High-attention items for Reviewer

### `el-elyon` — highest-lens-leverage entry in the Abraham cycle to date

This is the most theologically and politically delicate entry the Genesis translation has produced. **Reviewer should verify the entry's posture stays in the apparatus.** Specific items to check:

1. **The West-Semitic-pantheon framework reading is mainstream, not WoH-distinctive.** The entry should cite this framework as scholarly consensus (Cross 1973, Smith 2001), not as a lens-specific claim. Verify the rationale's framing is measured — "the framework already supplies the layered-divine-council picture the lens is interested in" — and does not over-reach.

2. **The Deut 32:8 textual variant is reported accurately.** The DSS reading at 4QDeut^j and 4QDeut^q reads *bnei ha-Elohim* (sons of Elohim — divine beings); LXX reads *aggelōn theou* (angels of God), reflecting an underlying *bnei Elohim*; MT reads *bnei Yisra'el*. The DSS / LXX reading is the modern scholarly consensus as the original reading (Skehan, BASOR 1959; Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible*, 3rd ed. 2012; Tigay, *JPS Deuteronomy*, 1996, Excursus 31; both NRSV and NJPS adopt the DSS reading). The entry should report this as direct textual data, **not** as a project-specific lens claim. Verify the entry frames it this way.

3. **The WoH translation choice for the divine name itself**: rendering 'El Elyon' as a bare transliteration rather than 'God Most High'. This follows the WoH convention of preserving Hebrew divine-name compounds (cf. the existing `el-shaddai` entry in the glossary at the patriarchal-name level). The choice is defensible and consistent with the prior translation passes; Reviewer should confirm it matches the existing convention.

### `yhwh-el-elyon-apposition` — the chapter's keystone editorial-syncretism move

Verify the entry's framing is accurate: the appositional reading is **consensus across LXX, Samaritan Pentateuch, Targum Onkelos, and Vulgate**. The harmonization-reading (that this is the Genesis editor's explicit identification of YHWH with the El Elyon of the Melchizedek tradition) is Westermann 1985 / Cross 1973 / Smith 2001 — mainstream modern critical scholarship. The textual variant (some Samaritan / Peshitta witnesses omitting *YHWH*) is direct manuscript evidence and is reported as such.

### `melchizedek`, `melekh-shalem-jerusalem`, `kohen-first-melchizedek`

The Melchizedek block (vv 18-20) is the chapter's most theologically loaded sequence. Three entries carry it. Verify:

- **`melchizedek`**: the theophoric (*malki* + *Tzedek*-as-deity-name) versus honorific (*king of righteousness*) name-readings are both presented; the entry does not commit to one. The Adoni-Tzedek parallel at Josh 10:1, 10:3 is reported as direct attestation of pre-Israelite Jerusalem *Tzedek*-theophoric king-names. Downstream-reception material (11QMelchizedek, Hebrews 5-7, rabbinic Shem-identification at b. Ned. 32b) is flagged as post-biblical and not preempted.

- **`melekh-shalem-jerusalem`**: the Jerusalem identification is anchored directly in Ps 76:3 and in 1QapGen col. 22 (with Josephus, Targum Onkelos, Hebrews 7:1-2 concurring); the minority independent-site and divine-epithet readings are reported but not adopted.

- **`kohen-first-melchizedek`**: the first-occurrence status, pre-Aaronic / pre-Sinaitic locus, combined king-priest office, and *l-El Elyon* (not *l-YHWH*) collocation are all directly verifiable from the text. The Hebrews 5-7 reception is flagged as post-biblical.

### `koneh-shamayim-va-aretz` — translation choice notable

The WoH translation reads 'possessor of heaven and earth' (matching ASV), **not** the modern-critical 'maker / creator of heaven and earth' (NRSV / NJPS / NIV / Alter). The choice is deliberate and the rationale is recorded in the glossary entry: the verse functions as a legitimacy-claim formula in context (Melchizedek's blessing, then Abram's oath), the *possess* sense is the dominant Hebrew sense of *q-n-h*, and the inherited West-Semitic *create*-sense material (Ugaritic *qny ʾrṣ*, Phoenician Karatepe *ʾl qn ʾrṣ*, Hittite-Hurrian *Elkunirsa*) is carried in the apparatus rather than the English. The choice can be revisited in a future editorial pass; flagging here so Reviewer can register whether the rationale is satisfactory or wants to move toward the modern-critical reading.
