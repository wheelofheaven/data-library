## Editorial decisions requiring sign-off

### `metzacheq-ishmael-debate` (v 9) — load-bearing

**Source:** מְצַחֵק (*metzacheq* — piel participle of *tzachaq*)

**WoH choice:** *playing*

**Why this matters:** The Translator's draft used ASV's *mocking* —
the Editor moved the translation to *playing*, following the modern
critical consensus (Sarna, Alter, NJPS, Westermann). The chapter has
four parallel readings each with substantial defense in the
philological literature, and the choice has heavy weight on the
Hagar/Yishma'el expulsion narrative.

The four readings:

- *(a) playing / sporting* (modern critical default; Sarna, Alter,
  NJPS, Westermann) — the participle as the deliberate continuation
  of the chapter's *tzachoq* / *yitzchaq* / *Yitzchaq* name-and-verb
  play at vv 6-8.
- *(b) playing-with-Isaac* (LXX expansion *meta Isaak tou huiou
  autēs*, followed by Vulgate, Peshitta, NRSV, presupposed in Paul's
  *diōkōn* at Gal 4:29).
- *(c) mocking / abusing* (ASV/KJV; the piel's harsher uses at Gen
  19:14, Gen 39:14-17, Exod 32:6).
- *(d) Rabbinic-tradition maximal* (Gen Rab 53:11; b. Sotah 26b;
  Rashi — idolatry / sexual immorality / bloodshed).

**Editorial principle.** The MT does not carry explicit harsher
content. The LXX expansion is treated as later expansion. The
modern critical scholarly consensus is for the *playing* default
reading. The editorial principle is to not import rabbinic-tradition
harshness on Yishma'el where the MT does not itself carry it.

**Recommendation:** ship as *playing*. The dispute is documented in
the glossary entry and per-verse commentary; the rabbinic-tradition
and Pauline-reception readings are flagged-not-preempted.

### `malakh-elohim-messenger` (v 17) — load-bearing

**Source:** מַלְאַךְ אֱלֹהִים (*mal'akh Elohim*)

**WoH choice:** *messenger of Elohim*

**Why this matters:** The first Hebrew Bible occurrence of *mal'akh
Elohim* — and editorially **distinct** from the *mal'akh YHWH* of
Gen 16. The chapter's second Hagar-scene uses *Elohim* / *mal'akh
Elohim* consistently (vv 12, 17 twice, 19, 20) where Gen 16 used
*YHWH* / *mal'akh YHWH* consistently. The Editor has authored a
parallel-but-distinct entry from the existing
`malakh-yhwh-messenger`, not a sub-variant.

**Source-critical framing.** Modern critical scholarship reads the
divine-name shift as the principal source-critical evidence for the
documentary attribution of the two Hagar-scenes to different sources:
Gen 16 to **J** (Yahwist), Gen 21:8-21 to **E** (Elohist), with the
two scenes constituting a doublet. The entry presents this as the
standard modern-critical treatment (Westermann 1985, Speiser 1964,
Friedman 1987, Skinner 1910) and also presents the post-documentary
critique (Van Seters 1975, Carr 1996, Schmid 1999) which preserves
the doublet-recognition while questioning the four-source
attribution. Neither position is preempted; the doublet itself is
the load-bearing scholarly datum.

**Editorial principle.** The translation preserves the divine-name
distinction visible to the English reader (*messenger of Elohim*,
not *angel of God*) parallel-but-distinct from *messenger of YHWH*.
The source-critical and theophanic-mediation distinctions are
carried in the glossary entry and per-verse commentary.

**Recommendation:** ship. The source-critical reading is documented
as the standard modern-critical treatment without committing the
project to a specific documentary-source position; the
post-documentary critique is also presented.

### `yhwh-el-olam` (v 33) — load-bearing

**Source:** יְהוָה אֵל עוֹלָם (*YHWH El Olam*)

**WoH choice:** *YHWH, the Everlasting El*

**Why this matters:** The first occurrence of *El Olam* in the
Hebrew Bible, completing the four-fold patriarchal El-compound
divine-naming pattern in the Avraham cycle (El Elyon Gen 14 → El
Ro'i Gen 16 → El Shaddai Gen 17 → El Olam Gen 21). The four
El-compounds across four distinct sanctuary-sites are
editorially-significant religious-historical data.

**Religious-historical framing.** The entry presents the standard
modern-critical reading: the patriarchal El-compound pattern
preserves a *West-Semitic El-religion locally diversified*, with
each sanctuary-site preserving a distinct El-compound divine name
corresponding to that site's pre-Israelite high-god cult; the
Yahwistic editor harmonizes each El-compound with YHWH through the
appositional construction (*YHWH El Elyon* at Gen 14:22; *YHWH El
Olam* at Gen 21:33). The reading is grounded in Alt *Der Gott der
Väter* 1929 (foundational), Cross *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic*
1973 ch. 1 (canonical modern statement), Smith 2001, Day 2000,
Köckert 1988 (critical-revisionist).

**The *olam* lexeme.** Read in the temporal-duration register
(*long duration, indefinite extent in time*) rather than the
abstract-eternity register; the latter is a later semantic
development. HALOT, BDB, TDOT (Preuss), Jenni 1952-53. The
translation preserves ASV's *Everlasting El*, rejecting the
NIV-style *Eternal God* overreading.

**Recommendation:** ship. The El-compound transliteration practice
is established (cf. `el-elyon`, `el-roi`, `el-shaddai`); the
religious-historical reconstruction is presented as the standard
modern-critical reading without preempting the wider
patriarchal-religion historiographical debates.

## Other notable entries

### `paqach-eynayim-revelatory-perception` (v 19)

The patriarchal corpus's first occurrence of the *paqach et
ha-eynayim* (open the eyes) revelatory-perception idiom. The entry
documents (i) the cross-corpus distribution across three registers
(revelatory-perception / recognition-of-state / eschatological-
restoration); (ii) the *well-was-always-there* modern critical
default (Sarna, Westermann, NJPS, Alter) vs. the *well-created-at-
the-moment* rabbinic-tradition reading (Gen Rab 53:14,
Pseudo-Jonathan, Tanchuma); (iii) the foreign-Egyptian-woman-as-seer
pattern (Trible 1984; Reis 2002). The WoH translation adopts the
modern critical default; the rabbinic reading is documented but not
adopted.

### `elohim-imkha-divine-presence-formula` (v 22)

The first patriarchal occurrence of the *Elohim/YHWH im-X* divine-
accompaniment formula, with cross-corpus distribution across 100+
verses. The entry distinguishes two structural functions (divine
self-disclosure vs. external recognition) per Preuss 1968 *BWANT
87*, the foundational monograph. The Avimelekh-recognition at v 22
is the chapter's structural pivot from the Hagar-narrative to the
Avimelekh-treaty narrative.

### `be-er-sheva-folk-etymology` (v 31)

The *sheva*-as-seven / *shava*-as-oath double-etymology, with the
Gen 26:33 doublet activating only the partial single-sense
etymology. The Tell es-Seba archaeological identification is
documented per Aharoni 1973 / Herzog 1984/2016. Cross-corpus
*Dan-to-Be'er-Sheva* boundary-formula documented but deferred to
those chapters' `appliesTo` expansions.

### `eshel-tamarisk-cultic-site` (v 33)

The *Tamarix aphylla* botanical identification (mainstream
consensus), with the three-fold HB hapax-cluster (Gen 21:33; 1 Sam
22:6; 1 Sam 31:13). The deliberate-cultic-tree-planting register is
editorially distinct from the pre-existing-sacred-tree register of
the wider patriarchal cycle (the *elon Moreh* of Gen 12:6, the
*elonei Mamre* of Gen 13:18, 18:1, etc.). The sacred-tree-cultic-
marker reading is documented per Pope *Song of Songs* 1985, Frevel
1995, Olyan 1988, Hadley 2000. The Editor judged `claim_type=direct`
on the grounds that the cross-corpus pattern is documentary; the
project-specific WoH synthesis on the sacred-tree-cult is reserved
for the wiki.
