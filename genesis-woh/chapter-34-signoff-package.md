## The chapter's character

Gen 34 is the project's most philologically and ethically charged chapter to date. The translation preserves five major lexical ambiguities in the English where mainstream scholarship is divided:

- **v 2 *innah*** — rape (Sternberg, Fewell-Gunn, Frymer-Kensky, Scholz, Kruger, NJPS) vs. status-degradation (Bechtel, Yamada, Wenham). ASV-baseline "humbled" preserves both.
- **v 7 *nevalah b-Yisra'el*** — technical sacrilegious-outrage formula (Phillips 1975 *VT* 25); first deployment of the compound (later: Judg 19-20; 2 Sam 13; Deut 22; Jer 29) — documentary-evidence of late editing.
- **v 13 *be-mirmah*** — Gen 27:35 lexical echo: Ya'aqov's own deception inherited by his sons.
- **v 25 *betach*** — victims-unaware (Sarna, Wenham, Speiser, ASV) vs. attackers-confident (Onqelos, Westermann, NJPS); the slaughter's grammar itself ambiguous.
- **v 31 *ha-khe-zonah*** — chapter-closing rhetorical question, deliberately unanswered. Sternberg / Alter / Fewell-Gunn / Fishbane / Fokkelman unanimous on the aporia. Gen 49:5-7 deathbed-curse retrospectively pre-empts.
