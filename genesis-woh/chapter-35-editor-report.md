This chapter closes the patriarchal-generation arc — Avraham → Yitzchaq → Ya'aqov complete; the twelve tribes complete at v 18; Yitzchaq's death and burial-by-reconciled-brothers (Esav + Ya'aqov) at v 29. ## Editorial decisions documented

### 1. The v 10 P-doublet handling — first-encounter pattern

This chapter is the project's first encounter with a clear documentary-doublet on a load-bearing renaming. Three options were considered:

*(a)* Extend the existing overlay entry `yisrael-name-aetiology-sarah-im-elohim` (Gen 32:29) to include GEN-WOH-35:10 in its appliesTo array, with a section on the P-doublet phenomenon added to its rationale.

*(b)* Promote the Penuel-etymology entry to the central glossary and expand its documentary-doublet coverage.

*(c)* Create a sibling overlay entry to the Penuel-etymology entry, covering specifically the v 10 P-doublet phenomenon, while preserving the Penuel-etymology entry intact for v 29 [sic — should be v 32:29; corrected here].

**Decision: option (c).** Rationale:

- The Penuel-etymology entry's body is densely-developed around the in-text etymology *ki sarita im-Elohim* and the five etymological positions (El-strives, one-who-strives-with-El, El-is-upright, El-rules, El-shines). The Gen 35:10 P-doublet is a different phenomenon — the documentary-source-doublet of a renaming-event — not an etymological-debate. Folding the doublet-phenomenon into the etymology-entry would dilute the focus of both.
- The sibling-overlay-entry preserves clean separation: one entry per phenomenon, both anchored to their respective verses.
- Promotion to central is premature: until Hosea 12:4-5 is translated (which would activate the Hosea-reception cross-corpus and may favor central-promotion of the etymology-entry), the Penuel-entry's cross-corpus reach remains HB-internal-singular and overlay-appropriate.
- The two-entry-pairing pattern may serve as a template for subsequent doublet-encounters (Gen 17 / Exod 6 Avraham-covenant framing; Gen 28 / Gen 35 Bethel-doublet — potentially also handled with two-entry-pairing).

This decision is recorded in the rationale of `yisrael-second-renaming-beit-el-p-doublet` (overlay v1.3.0).

### 2. Three-entry split for the v 22 Re'uven-Bilhah deed

The v 22 deed-narrative is the chapter's most-load-bearing interpretive crux, and the editorial decision was three-way:

- The **`pilegesh`** status-class lexeme has wide HB cross-corpus distribution (Gen, Judg, 1-2 Sam, 1 Kgs, Esth, Song, 1 Chr; >40 occurrences) and qualifies for central-glossary status under the cross-corpus-reach criterion.
- The **`piska b-emtza pasuq`** Masoretic textual phenomenon has cross-corpus reach (~28 HB instances) and qualifies for central-glossary status as a Masoretic-technique-entry covering the phenomenon broadly with Gen 35:22 as the canonical-exemplar.
- The **Re'uven-Bilhah deed and its Gen 49:3-4 retrospective** is chapter-specific (the deed occurs once; the retrospective binds it to a single deathbed-blessing). The integrated-interpretive crux belongs in the overlay.

This three-entry split preserves the cross-corpus / chapter-singular architectural-discipline. The same pattern was applied in the Gen 32 (Penuel) chapter where `gid-ha-nasheh-sciatic-nerve-dietary-rule` (chapter-singular dietary-aetiology) was housed in overlay while the surrounding lexical-and-philological material was housed in central entries.

### 3. The twelfth-tribe-completion entry — overlay placement

The Binyamin-Ben-Oni naming-aetiology is the twelfth and final tribal-eponym naming in the Genesis sequence. Two options were considered:

*(a)* Overlay entry as a chapter-singular completion-of-the-pattern observation.

*(b)* Central entry with cross-link to the existing four-cluster central entries (`four-leah-firstborn-name-etymologies-cluster`, `bilhah-zilpah-handmaid-sons-aetiology-cluster`, `leah-second-sons-aetiology-cluster`, `yosef-double-name-aetiology`).

**Decision: option (a).** Rationale:

- The Binyamin-naming is a single-name aetiology with chapter-singular completion-significance, not a multi-name cluster like the other four entries. Including it as a sibling to the cluster-entries would create a category-mismatch (a single-name entry alongside multi-name cluster entries).
- The Binyamin-naming has a distinctive mother-vs-father naming-dispute structure (Rachel names *Ben-Oni*; Ya'aqov renames *Binyamin*) that the cluster-entries do not have. The mother-vs-father naming-pattern is chapter-specific.
- The completion-of-the-twelve structural observation is chapter-specific to Gen 35:18: the verse is *the* twelfth-tribe naming-aetiology, and the editorial-completion-function is bound to its position in Gen 35.
- The cross-corpus reach (Gen 49:27 deathbed-blessing; 1 Sam 10:2 Rachel's-Tomb in Binyamin-territory; the Mari *Banū-Yamīna* parallel) is HB-and-ANE-internal but is bound to the Binyamin-tribe's identity, not to a cross-corpus lexical-or-philological pattern.

### 4. The *elohei ha-nekhar* central-glossary placement

The lexeme has substantial cross-corpus reach (Gen 35:2, 4; Josh 24:23-24; Judg 10:16; 1 Sam 7:3; Jer 5:19; 2 Chr 33:15) and is editorially-load-bearing as the Deuteronomistic-historian's covenant-renewal-precondition formula. **Central placement** is unambiguous. The decision criteria:

- The formula recurs across at least six HB books — far exceeding the cross-corpus threshold.
- The modern critical reading of household-polytheism (Halpern, M. Smith, Albertz) is mainstream and is shared across multiple books' commentary literature.
- Future translations of Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Jeremiah, and Chronicles will all benefit from the central entry's documentation.

The lens-discipline observation: the mainstream-modern-critical reading of editorially-foregrounded Israelite household polytheism is *not* WoH-distinctive synthesis. The entry presents the mainstream reading and explicitly notes (in the closing of the rationale) that project-specific synthesis is reserved for the wiki and not preempted by the entry.

### 5. The Re'uven-Bilhah deed lens-discipline observation

The mainstream modern critical reading of the deed as a succession-claim power-grab is documented in Sarna, Westermann, Hamilton, Speiser, Alter, Westbrook, Tigay, and van Seters. The HB-internal parallels (2 Sam 16:21-22, 1 Kgs 2:13-25, 2 Sam 3:7) and the ANE-comparative legal material (HL §195, MAL §41, CH §157) provide the documentary-support. This reading is not WoH-distinctive synthesis.

Any project-specific WoH-distinctive reading (a particular framing of the deed within the patriarchal-narrative's wider context, or a particular reading of the *aqav*-character's deception-and-displacement pattern) is reserved for the wiki and is not preempted by the overlay entry.
