## The chapter's signature additions

### `malakh-yhwh-messenger` (the load-bearing entry)

First occurrence of the messenger-of-YHWH figure in the entire Hebrew Bible (v 7), recurring 60+ times across the canon (Gen 21:17, 22:11, 31:11, 48:16; Exod 3:2, 14:19; Num 22; Josh 5:13-15; Judg 2, 6, 13; 2 Sam 24; 1 Kgs 19; 2 Kgs 1, 19; Isa 37, 63; Zech 1-3; and many more). The entry presents **all three modern critical positions** in genuine parallel:
- (a) **distinct-envoy** reading (Heidt 1949; classic Christian-tradition)
- (b) **identity** reading (Westermann, von Rad — supported by Exod 3:2 where the *ma'lakh* speaks AS YHWH)
- (c) **literary-theological-device** reading (Hamori 2008, Sommer 2009, MacDonald 2009)

Translation choice: **"messenger of YHWH"** (departing from ASV's "angel of Jehovah" — *angel* imports later Christian-angelology that doesn't belong in the Hebrew). The Reviewer verified: "No project-specific WoH synthesis injected. No later-Christian-angelology imports. Defensible at the highest scholarly bar."

### `pere-adam-wild-ass-prophecy` (the most-sensitive entry)

v 12's *pere adam* prophecy about Ishmael. The entry navigates a fraught reception history:
- Mainstream HB scholarly reading: wilderness-pastoralist (Westermann, Sarna, Wenham, Hamilton)
- Islamic-tradition reception (Ishmael as ancestor of the Arabs; Sura 14:39, 19:54-55; Genesis Rabbah) — **flagged as downstream, not preempted**
- 19th-20th c. Orientalist racialization — **explicitly named and rejected** with Said 1978 *Orientalism* citation
- Explicit guard-rail sentence in the entry: *"This glossary entry must not ship implicit racialization."*

### `el-roi-and-halom-elohim-variant`

v 13's *El Roi* — **the first divine-name-naming by a woman in the Hebrew Bible** (Trible 1984 *Texts of Terror*, Reis 2002). The MT reading *halom* (here/now) is preserved; the LXX/Vulgate emendation to *Elohim* ("seen God") is noted as a textual variant with the seeing-God-and-living parallels (Exod 33:20, Judg 6:22-23, Judg 13:22).
