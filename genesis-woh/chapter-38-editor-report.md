## The major crux: *zonah* (v 15) vs *qedeshah* (vv 21–22) — lens-discipline application

The narrator calls the disguised Tamar a *zonah* (common prostitute, v 15); Hirah, asking the locals after her, switches to *qedeshah* (vv 21–22), a feminine agent-noun from q-d-sh ("set apart / consecrated"). The conventional rendering "cult / temple / sacred prostitute" rests on the contested institution of ancient-Near-Eastern sacred prostitution.

Both named positions are documented in the central entry `qedeshah-consecrated-woman-sacred-prostitution-crux` and in the v 21 commentary, neither adjudicated:

- **Traditional cultic reading** — *qadesh*/*qedeshah* as sanctuary cult-functionaries with a ritual-sexual role (Albright; Speiser; the Deut 23:18-19 and Hos 4:14 cultic pairings; the 1–2 Kgs reform-notices; Herodotus 1.199 / Lucian analogies).
- **Revisionist denial** — institutional "sacred prostitution" as a modern scholarly construct with no secure ancient basis; *qedeshah* = a consecrated cult-servant, here perhaps a polite upgrade of *zonah* (S. L. Budin, *The Myth of Sacred Prostitution in Antiquity*, 2008; J. G. Westenholz, HTR 82 [1989] 245-265; M. I. Gruber, UF 18 [1986] 133-148; P. A. Bird 1997; Frymer-Kensky 1992).

**Surface decision for *qedeshah*: transliterate ("qedeshah").** This preserves the deliberate *zonah* → *qedeshah* lexical shift (which "harlot/prostitute" would erase and "cult prostitute" would over-commit), flags the crux, and leaves the cultic question open — the She'ol-type transliteration-of-the-irreducible-term precedent. **Home: central**, by decisive cross-corpus reach (Deut 23:18; Hos 4:14; 1 Kgs 14:24; 15:12; 22:47; 2 Kgs 23:7). Flagged as carrying the cross-corpus governing decision when Deuteronomy / Hosea / Kings are translated.

Standard, uncontested rendering of the Niphal of *saraf*; commentary notes the gravity of burning as an execution-mode (Lev 20:14; 21:9) and the irony (Yehudah, who solicited the "prostitute," pronounces the sentence).
