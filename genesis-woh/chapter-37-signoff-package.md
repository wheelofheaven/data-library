## Items requiring decision (2)

Both Reviewer `flag-for-human` verdicts are **project-stance calls, not philological failures** — the Editor's renderings are sound; the flags ask you to ratify a surface decision a citation can't settle.

### 1. v3 — *ketonet passim* ("coat of many colours")

The surface keeps the culturally-entrenched ASV **"coat of many colours."** The entry itself concedes this is the philologically *weakest* of three readings: the 2 Sam 13:18-19 parallel (the phrase's only other HB occurrence, Tamar's royal virgin's garment) and LXX/Speiser favour a **long-sleeved / ornamented status-tunic.** The correction is carried honestly in the glossary + commentary.

- **Editor's recommendation (and mine):** keep "coat of many colours" on the surface (accuracy-above-lens / ASV-baseline conservatism — this is the most recognized phrase in the chapter), with the stronger status-reading documented in apparatus. This matches how prior chapters handle entrenched ASV renderings against better philology.
- **Alternative:** surface the status-tunic reading ("a long ornamented tunic") and demote "many colours" to the apparatus.

### 2. v36 — Pharaoh vs Par'oh

The original Gen 37 brief listed "Par'oh" in the transliteration set, but every prior chapter (12–20) renders **"Pharaoh."** The Translator kept "Pharaoh" and flagged the conflict; the Editor confirmed it stands.

- **Editor's recommendation (and mine):** **"Pharaoh" stands** corpus-wide, per the established chs-12–20 stable text. The brief's "Par'oh" is deferred to any future deliberate corpus-wide retransliteration pass (not a per-chapter decision).
- **Alternative:** begin rendering "Par'oh" here, accepting a corpus inconsistency until older chapters are normalized.
