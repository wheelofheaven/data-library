# Chapter 1 Editor Report — Enki and Ninhursag (ETCSL 1.1.1)

## Overview

The Wheel of Heaven Translation Program's **third Mesopotamian text** and the canonical Sumerian **Dilmun-paradise** composition — the comparative-mythology pair to Hebrew Eden (Gen 2-3). **256 lines preserved out of 281** (~91%) across six small in-line lacunae (3, 9, 4, 2, 4, 3 lines missing/fragmentary at lines 40, 142, 164, 190, 235, 245). The Translator submitted **30 editorial_questions**; this Editor pass resolves all of them (either folded into a glossary entry, applied via existing central entry, or surfaced in per-line commentary) and clears the array.

This pass is the project's **most comparative-mythology-rich Mesopotamian Editor pass to date** for Sumerian-Hebrew cross-corpus material, anchored by the **Nin-ti / Eve rib-life philological pun** (Kramer 1944 *BASOR* 96) and the **Dilmun-Eden** parallel. Operational disavowals on both the Dilmun-Eden and the Nin-ti / Eve clusters are spelled out throughout the new central entries' rationale prose.

## Lens-discipline notes for human reviewer

The chapter is the project's **most-direct meeting** of the Mesopotamian paradise-tradition with the Hebrew Eden-tradition. The Editor pass took two lens-discipline measures throughout:

1. **All cross-corpus comparative-mythology observations are framed as structural-mythological-motif or shared rhetorical-figure, never as etymological-cognation or genealogical-descent.** The Sumerian and Hebrew material is treated as independent linguistic developments converging on shared narrative-templates, documented in named comparative-mythology scholarship (Kramer; Hallo; Sarna; Wenham; Westermann; Heidel; Cassuto).

2. **Explicit operational disavowals are spelled out at length in three places**: (i) the Dilmun-Eden central entry (extended in the rationale prose); (ii) the Nin-ti / Eve central entry (three numbered disavowals); (iii) the per-line commentary at line 269 (the Nin-ti / Eve crux). The disavowals are: no etymological-identity claim; no Christian-Eve-typology pre-resolution; no project-specific 'engineered-paradise', 'Eve-as-Sumerian-borrowed', 'Eden-as-spaceport', 'DUR.AN.KI', 'Anunnaki-installation', or similar synthesis-readings.

The surface English prose stays in scholarly modern register throughout; no WoH synthesis is visible on the surface or in glossary entry rationale. The lens, where relevant to the wider project, lives in the wiki / methodology pages — not in this translation or its apparatus.
