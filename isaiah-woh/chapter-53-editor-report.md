## Lens-discipline records (for the reviewer's attention)

### 1. The servant's identity — the governing crux (`eved-servant-identity-isaiah`, @ISA-53:11)

**Decision:** the text renders the servant language strictly plainly ("he / him / my servant") and **identifies no referent**. Commentary lays out all readings as hedged comparative/critical layers: the mainstream Jewish corporate-Israel / faithful-remnant reading (grounded in Isa 49:3 and the medieval exegetes); the individual-prophet / Deutero-Isaiah reading (Duhm's Servant-Songs isolation); the Christian messianic / vicarious-atonement reading and its NT reception (Acts 8:32–35; 1 Pet 2:22–25); and, lightly, the comparative righteous-sufferer-vindicated resonance. **No Wheel of Heaven identification of the servant is asserted anywhere** — in the line or in the apparatus. This is the deliberate accuracy-before-lens ruling for the chapter; the Hebrew names no referent and neither do we.

### 2. `mukkēh ʾĕlōhîm` "smitten of God" at 53:4 (`elohim-as-god-observers-53-4-isaiah`, @ISA-53:4)

**Decision:** `ʾĕlōhîm` is rendered **"God"** at the standard surface, and the corpus "the Elohim" convention (central entry `elohim-as-translation`) is **deliberately NOT applied** to this line. Reason: the clause reports the onlookers' *mistaken* judgment ("we accounted him … struck down by God"), which 53:5 immediately overturns ("but he was pierced because of *our* transgressions"). Reading "struck down by the Elohim" would import a lens-claim onto the one line the poem stages in order to reject it. Consequently **53:4 is NOT added to the central `elohim-as-translation` appliesTo** — that entry's own `wohChoice` is "the Elohim," and it cannot govern a line whose rendering is "God."

### Reference-authority caveat (Editor note, not a blocker)

The task brief listed **Wildberger** among the Isaiah-53 authorities. Wildberger's *Isaiah* commentary (Continental) covers **Isaiah 1–39 only** and does not treat chapter 53; citing him on this chapter would be a fabricated reference. I therefore used the Deutero-Isaiah authorities the brief also named — **Blenkinsopp** (*Isaiah 40–55*, AB 19A, 2002), **Baltzer** (*Deutero-Isaiah*, Hermeneia), **Goldingay & Payne** (ICC *Isaiah 40–55*), and **North** (*The Suffering Servant in Deutero-Isaiah*) — plus BDB/HALOT and the primary text-critical data (1QIsaᵃ, 1QIsaᵇ, LXX). Wildberger is correctly retained only in the already-shipped chapter 6 material. Flagging for the reviewer in case the brief intended a different Isaiah-53 volume.
